**Project input checklist**

1. **Site information** — Google Maps pin, cadastral plan, plot boundaries, survey or satellite image.
2. **Project purpose** — Academic design project, government proposal, NGO project or actual investment.
3. **Hospital capacity** — Expected consultations, emergency services, observation or inpatient beds, if known.
4. **Site area** — Verified land area, or allow the feasibility stage to establish the requirement.
5. **Budget** — Budget in COP, funding source or a requirement for preliminary cost scenarios.
6. **Local planning data** — Land-use classification, terrain information, existing healthcare facilities and population data.
7. **Drawing requirements** — Required plans, scales, presentation sheets and software.

**Recommended workflow**

1. Establish the need and select the site: verify healthcare access, compare candidate parcels and check land-use and environmental restrictions. This is the most important step because the exact plot is not yet established.
2. Establish the clinical brief and area programme: determine services, room quantities, room areas, patient flows and operational requirements before fixing the building size.
3. Develop and compare architectural concepts: alternative site layouts, massing studies, circulation diagrams and functional concepts before selecting a design.
4. Develop coordinated drawings and technical systems: plans, sections, elevations, structure, services, accessibility, infection control, sustainability and preliminary costs.

> **Recommendation:** Start with the feasibility stage, not the floor plans. For Pasquilla–Mochuelo, the architectural concept should follow the evidence on healthcare access, suitable land and the appropriate service level. Otherwise, you risk producing an impressive hospital design on a site that may not be suitable or a facility that duplicates existing services.

# MASTER PROMPT: COMPLETE ARCHITECTURAL DESIGN PROPOSAL FOR A COMMUNITY HOSPITAL IN BOGOTÁ, COLOMBIA

## 1. Your Role

Act as an integrated multidisciplinary architectural and healthcare consultancy comprising: Principal Architect; Healthcare Architect and Hospital Planner; Urban Designer and Regional Planner; Site Selection and Feasibility Consultant; Healthcare Operations Planner; Medical Planning Consultant; Structural Engineer; Geotechnical Engineer; Civil and Infrastructure Engineer; Mechanical, Electrical and Plumbing (MEP) Engineer; Medical Gas Systems Consultant; Fire and Life Safety Consultant; Infection Prevention and Control Consultant; Environmental and Sustainability Consultant; Landscape Architect; Accessibility Consultant; Quantity Surveyor and Cost Estimator; BIM Coordinator; CAD Documentation Specialist; Architectural Visualisation Artist; Colombian Building Regulations Researcher; Healthcare Project Manager.

Your task is to develop a comprehensive, technically reasoned, evidence-based architectural design proposal for a proposed community hospital in Bogotá, Colombia.

Do not produce merely a conceptual description, generic hospital layout, mood board or collection of architectural renders. Develop a coordinated proposal progressing from project justification and site feasibility to the architectural brief, area programming, conceptual design, functional planning, technical systems, cost assessment and final presentation.

Distinguish verified facts, preliminary assumptions, professional recommendations and items requiring confirmation by local authorities or consultants.

**Project title:** Proposed Community Hospital for Improved Healthcare Access in Pasquilla–Mochuelo, Ciudad Bolívar, Bogotá, Colombia.

**Project location**

- Country: Colombia
- City: Bogotá, Capital District
- Locality: Ciudad Bolívar
- Preliminary study area: Pasquilla–Mochuelo rural–urban transition
- General site context: Southern Bogotá, including rural settlements and surrounding neighbourhoods

  - Mochuelo: <https://www.google.com/maps/search/Mochuelo,+Ciudad+Bolivar,+Bogota,+Colombia>

**Critical site-selection qualification**

No exact plot has been selected or verified. The project area is a candidate for investigation, not an officially confirmed hospital site.

Do not invent: a cadastral parcel or property boundary; exact site coordinates; land ownership; plot area or dimensions; existing utility capacity; officially established healthcare shortages; population figures without a source; hospital travel times without a network analysis; zoning permissions or environmental approvals; confirmation that the proposed land is vacant or buildable.

First establish whether the area demonstrates a measurable healthcare accessibility gap and whether a suitable parcel can legally and physically accommodate the proposed facility.

If an exact plot cannot be established, develop a preliminary site-selection framework and clearly label any hypothetical site used for concept development.

**Project purpose**

Investigate and develop an appropriate healthcare facility that could improve access to primary healthcare, diagnostics, maternal and child healthcare, urgent assessment and emergency stabilisation for residents in underserved parts of the study area, subject to evidence of local demand.

**Initial facility hypothesis**

Evaluate a community-oriented, primary-care-led healthcare facility with urgent-care capability. Consider, subject to healthcare demand and referral arrangements: general outpatient consultation; nursing and preventive healthcare; maternal and child health; basic laboratory services; essential diagnostic imaging; pharmacy; minor procedures and treatment; emergency assessment and stabilisation; observation beds if justified; ambulance access and referral coordination; telemedicine and community outreach.

Do not automatically propose a large tertiary hospital, major surgical centre, intensive care unit, or inpatient ward. Establish the appropriate service level from documented demand, the existing hospital network, referral times, staffing, operational viability and regulatory requirements.

## 5. Phase 1 — Project Justification and Healthcare Need

Establish why the proposed project may be necessary.

**5.1 Regional healthcare context.** Investigate: Ciudad Bolívar's urban and rural settlement patterns; distribution of residents and population density; existing healthcare facilities and their actual service levels; public and private providers; existing primary care centres, clinics and hospitals; available outpatient, emergency, diagnostic, maternity and inpatient services; public transport connectivity; road network and ambulance access; geographic barriers and difficult terrain; socioeconomic and demographic characteristics relevant to healthcare access; existing referral pathways.

**5.2 Healthcare accessibility analysis.** Create an inventory of existing healthcare facilities using official or otherwise reliable data. For each facility, record, where available: name; coordinates; facility type; service capability; operating hours; emergency capability; bed capacity; public or private status; source and verification date.

Analyse access using road-network travel times, not straight-line distances alone. If adequate data are available, compare walking, public transport, private vehicle and ambulance access; peak and off-peak travel; and access during adverse weather or road disruption, where evidence permits. Map population clusters and identify areas with poor access to the relevant level of healthcare. Do not assume that the nearest building provides the required service. Distinguish proximity from actual clinical capability.

**5.3 Service gap assessment.** Determine whether the principal need is for:

- A. A primary healthcare centre.
- B. A primary healthcare facility with urgent stabilisation.
- C. A community hospital with limited inpatient care.
- D. An alternative intervention, such as upgrading an existing facility, mobile healthcare or improving transport and referral systems.

Compare these options using transparent criteria: unmet demand; accessibility improvement; clinical scope; workforce availability; capital expenditure; operating expenditure; referral efficiency; environmental impact; implementation feasibility.

**Required deliverable.** A project justification report with an evidence table, a healthcare facility map, a preliminary accessibility analysis and a reasoned recommendation on the appropriate facility type. If available evidence does not demonstrate the need for a new hospital, explicitly recommend further study or an alternative intervention rather than manufacture a justification.

## 6. Phase 2 — Site Selection and Feasibility

Compare several candidate locations within the Pasquilla–Mochuelo study area. Do not select a precise parcel until reliable geographic, legal and physical information is available.

**6.1 Site-selection criteria.** Assess: healthcare accessibility gap; catchment population; road access; ambulance access; public transport; pedestrian accessibility; land-use compatibility; land ownership and acquisition feasibility; plot geometry; developable area; terrain and slope; geotechnical stability; flood and landslide susceptibility; drainage; water availability; wastewater infrastructure; electricity supply; telecommunications; environmental protection restrictions; nearby incompatible uses; air quality and noise; construction logistics; emergency access and evacuation; scope for future expansion.

**6.2 GIS and spatial analysis.** Where data are available, use QGIS or equivalent GIS tools to prepare: study-area location map; existing healthcare facility distribution map; population density map; healthcare catchment and accessibility map; road and public transport map; terrain and slope map; environmental constraint map; land-use and zoning map; flood and landslide hazard screening map; candidate parcel comparison map. Identify dataset resolution, date, coordinate reference system and limitations. Do not infer parcel-level suitability from coarse regional data.

**6.3 Weighted site-selection matrix.** Develop a transparent multi-criteria decision matrix. For each criterion, provide: weight; scoring scale; evidence used; score for each candidate; weighted score; confidence level; reason for the score. Explain the weighting method and test whether reasonable changes in weights alter the preferred site. Do not use invented precision. If evidence is unavailable, mark the score as unknown and identify the information required.

**Required deliverable.** A comparative site-selection report identifying: preferred candidate, if evidence supports one; alternative candidate; reasons for selection; critical unresolved constraints; surveys, approvals and investigations needed before site confirmation.

## 7. Phase 3 — Site and Climate Analysis

After establishing a verified or explicitly hypothetical site, develop a detailed architectural site analysis. Investigate: geographic coordinates and elevation; site boundaries and adjoining properties; north direction; contours and slope gradients; existing vegetation and structures; neighbourhood character; road hierarchy; access points; pedestrian movement; public transport stops; existing views; noise sources; solar exposure; wind conditions; rainfall and drainage; temperature and humidity; local microclimate; water availability; environmental sensitivities; construction access; emergency vehicle movements.

For Bogotá's high-altitude Andean setting, investigate relevant temperature variation, rainfall patterns, solar radiation, orientation, wind exposure and thermal comfort. Do not apply a generic tropical or hot-climate strategy without checking local climate data.

**Required analytical drawings:** (1) regional context map; (2) local context map; (3) site location plan; (4) site photographs or documented visual references; (5) existing land-use map; (6) access and movement diagram; (7) contour and slope analysis; (8) sun and shadow analysis; (9) climate response diagram; (10) environmental constraints diagram; (11) opportunities and constraints diagram. If exact survey data are unavailable, clearly label diagrams as preliminary.

## 8. Phase 4 — Client Objectives and Design Brief

Develop a clear statement of the client's objectives. Separate: clinical, patient experience, staff operational, community, architectural, environmental, financial, regulatory and future expansion objectives. Convert each objective into a measurable or verifiable design response.

Create an **objective-to-design matrix** containing: (1) client objective; (2) identified problem; (3) design response; (4) performance indicator; (5) verification method.

**Proposed design principles.** Investigate the suitability of: patient-centred design; dignified and non-institutional environments; clear wayfinding; separation of public and clinical circulation where required; infection prevention; universal accessibility; privacy and confidentiality; staff wellbeing; low-maintenance materials; resilience and continuity of care; flexible clinical spaces; future expansion; appropriate integration with the surrounding landscape.

Do not assume all principles require separate departments or expensive architectural features. Prioritise operationally meaningful interventions.

## 9. Phase 5 — Healthcare Service Model and Capacity

Develop a clinically coherent service model before fixing the floor area.

**9.1 Define the service level.** Recommend a facility category based on: population demand; disease burden, where reliable data exist; existing healthcare provision; referral arrangements; clinical staffing; operating costs; regulatory classification; required diagnostic and treatment services.

**9.2 Define clinical capacity.** Estimate, with explicit assumptions: daily outpatient consultations; peak hourly arrivals; nursing and preventive care workload; maternal and child health demand; laboratory workload; imaging demand; emergency arrivals; observation requirements; staff numbers and shift patterns; waiting and support requirements. Where sufficient data are available, use transparent calculations (for example, consultation rooms from daily appointments, room utilisation, working hours and average consultation duration). For emergency and observation spaces, use appropriate healthcare planning methods rather than simply multiplying population by a generic bed ratio.

**9.3 Healthcare departments.** Evaluate the inclusion and scale of:

- **A. Public and administrative:** entrance lobby; reception and registration; waiting areas; patient information; administration; staff facilities; public toilets; security.
- **B. Outpatient care:** general consultation rooms; examination rooms; nursing assessment; preventive healthcare; maternal and child health; immunisation; counselling; telemedicine; treatment and minor procedures.
- **C. Urgent care:** triage; emergency assessment; resuscitation capability only if justified; treatment bays; observation spaces if justified; ambulance drop-off; emergency support areas.
- **D. Diagnostics:** sample collection; basic laboratory; imaging, if clinically and economically justified; diagnostic support and reporting.
- **E. Pharmacy:** dispensing; secure storage; temperature-controlled storage where required; staff work area.
- **F. Clinical support:** clean utility; dirty utility; clinical waste holding; medical supplies; equipment storage; housekeeping; linen storage; staff changing.
- **G. Building support:** electrical and backup power; water storage and treatment, if required; medical gases, if clinically required; IT and communications; mechanical plant; wastewater systems; general and biomedical waste management.
- **H. External areas:** ambulance bay; patient drop-off; accessible parking; staff parking, if appropriate; pedestrian access; service access; landscape; outdoor waiting or therapeutic spaces where suitable; future expansion zone.

For each department, explain why it is included, excluded or deferred. Do not include operating theatres, intensive care, major inpatient wards or specialised treatment facilities unless the service model, staffing, infrastructure, economics and regulatory requirements justify them.

## 10. Phase 6 — Detailed Architectural Area Programme

Create a comprehensive room-by-room schedule in square metres, distinguishing: net room area; departmental net area; internal circulation; walls and shafts; building support area; gross floor area; external site area.

For every space, include: (1) department; (2) room name; (3) number of rooms; (4) area per room; (5) total net area; (6) occupancy or users; (7) primary activities; (8) adjacency requirements; (9) privacy requirements; (10) accessibility requirements; (11) infection-control requirements; (12) water and drainage requirements; (13) electrical and IT requirements; (14) HVAC requirements; (15) medical gas requirements, if applicable; (16) furniture and equipment; (17) regulatory or technical references; (18) design assumptions.

Calculate departmental subtotals and the total building area. Show the calculation of gross floor area from the net programme, explain any circulation, service or grossing allowances and avoid counting the same area twice.

Provide at least two scenarios: a compact, primary-care-led facility; and an expanded community facility, if the demand analysis supports it. Do not select an arbitrary total floor area before developing the programme.

**Required deliverable.** A detailed area schedule that can be transferred to a spreadsheet and used for subsequent architectural planning.

## 11. Phase 7 — Functional Relationships and Circulation

**11.1 Adjacency analysis.** Create a room and department adjacency matrix identifying: essential adjacency; preferred adjacency; separation requirements; controlled access; public and staff-only zones; clean and dirty workflows; emergency connections; service and delivery routes.

**11.2 Bubble diagrams.** Prepare at least three alternative functional concepts. Each must demonstrate: public zone; outpatient zone; urgent-care zone; diagnostic zone; pharmacy; staff and administration; clinical support; service access; future expansion. Explain the advantages and disadvantages of each.

**11.3 Circulation planning.** Develop separate movement studies for: outpatients; emergency patients; ambulances; staff; visitors; supplies; clean materials; soiled materials; biomedical waste; food and deliveries, if applicable. Provide route diagrams and explain where separation is essential and where shared circulation is acceptable. Do not claim complete circulation separation unless the actual plan demonstrates it.

**Required deliverable.** An adjacency matrix, three bubble diagrams, a preferred functional concept and circulation diagrams.

## 12. Phase 8 — Concept Development

Develop an architectural concept that responds to the verified site, clinical programme, local context and environmental conditions. Explore at least three design concepts, for example a courtyard-based facility, a compact pavilion or clustered clinical blocks, or a linear healthcare spine with controlled expansion. Treat these as options, not predetermined solutions.

For each concept, explain: design idea; site response; massing strategy; functional organisation; daylight and ventilation; patient experience; staff efficiency; landscape integration; structural implications; construction complexity; future expansion; cost implications. Select the preferred concept using an explicit evaluation matrix.

**Design narrative.** Write: (1) a one-sentence concept statement; (2) a short architectural narrative; (3) three to five design principles; (4) a diagrammatic explanation of how the concept translates into form; (5) a description of the patient arrival experience; (6) a description of the clinical workflow; (7) a description of the relationship between building and landscape. Avoid superficial metaphors that do not inform the actual design.

## 13. Phase 9 — Site Planning and Massing

If no surveyed parcel is available, develop a hypothetical test-fit plan and state the assumed dimensions, slope and orientation prominently.

**Massing studies.** Develop at least three massing alternatives and compare: building height; footprint; solar exposure; views; circulation; privacy; construction feasibility; earthworks; environmental effects; future extension; site coverage. Explain the selected option.

## 14. Phase 10 — Floor Plan Development

Produce coordinated preliminary floor plans for every required level. For each, include: drawing title; scale; north arrow; grid or structural logic; room names and numbers; dimensions; room areas; door positions; windows; walls and partitions; corridors; accessible circulation; stairs and lifts, if required; sanitary facilities; furniture; clinical equipment; main entrances; emergency exits; departmental boundaries; relevant service spaces.

Provide: (1) ground-floor plan; (2) upper-floor plan, only if justified; (3) roof plan; (4) furniture and equipment layout; (5) functional zoning plan; (6) patient circulation plan; (7) staff circulation plan; (8) emergency circulation plan; (9) clean and dirty workflow diagrams; (10) expansion plan.

Make the floor plans consistent with the approved area schedule and room relationships. Do not generate arbitrary room arrangements without explaining how patients, clinicians, supplies and waste move through the building. For a small facility, evaluate a single-storey arrangement before proposing additional floors, considering terrain, accessibility, land availability, operational efficiency and expansion requirements.

If you cannot produce precise CAD drawings directly, provide dimensioned schematic plans, coordinates or a clearly specified drafting method suitable for implementation in CAD or BIM software. Do not misrepresent a conceptual diagram as a construction drawing.

## 15. Phase 11 — Elevations, Sections and Architectural Character

**Elevations.** Prepare descriptions or drawings for the main entrance, rear, side and service elevations, including: openings; shading; entrance canopy; material transitions; building proportions; accessibility; weather protection; signage; landscape relationship.

**Sections.** Prepare at least two important building sections demonstrating: ground relationship; slope response; floor-to-floor heights; ceiling and service zones; daylight; ventilation; roof drainage; structural logic; accessibility; relationships between clinical departments.

**Architectural language.** Develop a coherent material and façade strategy appropriate to Bogotá's climate and the project's maintenance budget. Evaluate: durable wall systems; weather-resistant external finishes; insulated roof and envelope options; high-performance windows; solar control; glazing privacy; acoustic control; easy-to-clean clinical finishes; locally available materials; maintenance and replacement cycles. Avoid specifying products or construction systems without confirming local availability, technical performance and compliance.

## 16. Phase 12 — Colombian Regulatory Compliance

Research the current applicable legal and technical framework. Verify, as relevant: seismic-resistant construction regulations, including the applicable NSR framework and amendments; planning and building licensing requirements; land-use and development restrictions; healthcare service provider registration and service-enablement requirements; healthcare infrastructure and equipment standards; accessibility; fire and life safety; occupational health and safety; environmental approvals; water supply and sanitation; biomedical waste management; medical gas systems; electrical installations; emergency evacuation; structural safety; local development and environmental constraints.

For each relevant requirement, provide: regulation or standard; current version or status; issuing authority; relevant provision; design implication; verification source; professional responsible for confirmation.

## 18. Phase 14 — MEP and Clinical Building Systems

- **IT and communications:** network infrastructure; electronic health records; cybersecurity; telemedicine; nurse call systems where required; access control; CCTV subject to privacy requirements; communications resilience.

**Equipment schedule.** Show: equipment name; quantity; room; space requirement; utility requirements; maintenance access; indicative procurement considerations. ## 19. Phase 15 — Infection Control and Patient Safety

## 20. Phase 16 — Environmental Sustainability and Landscape

Evaluate: passive thermal design; envelope insulation; daylight without excessive heat loss or glare; solar shading; natural ventilation where clinically appropriate; energy-efficient mechanical systems; water conservation; rainwater harvesting, subject to local regulations and intended use; efficient lighting; renewable energy feasibility; construction waste reduction; durable materials; low-maintenance landscaping; biodiversity protection; stormwater management; erosion control; landscape-based patient wellbeing.

Develop a landscape plan showing: existing vegetation; proposed planting; accessible outdoor spaces; shaded waiting; therapeutic gardens where suitable; pedestrian routes; stormwater treatment; ecological protection; maintenance requirements. Quantify projected energy or water savings only where a transparent calculation or credible model supports the estimate.

## 21. Phase 17 — Universal Accessibility and Wayfinding

## 28. Required Working Method

Work sequentially rather than generating an entire unverified hospital proposal in one step.

- **Stage 1 — Evidence and feasibility:** project definition, source research, healthcare accessibility assessment, site-selection criteria and preliminary service model.
- **Stage 2 — Design brief:** clinical capacity, departmental requirements, room schedule, area calculations and adjacency requirements.
- **Stage 3 — Concept design:** three concepts, compared, with one recommended.
- **Stage 4 — Schematic design:** site planning, massing, floor plans, circulation, sections and elevations.
- **Stage 5 — Technical coordination:** structural, MEP, accessibility, infection-control, sustainability and regulatory strategies.
- **Stage 6 — Cost and delivery:** cost model, project phasing, risk register and implementation recommendations.
- **Stage 7 — Final presentation:** report, drawing list, visualisation brief, compliance matrix and design audit.

At the end of each stage: summarise the decisions made; list evidence used; list assumptions; identify missing inputs; identify decisions requiring client approval; identify specialist investigations; state whether the next stage can proceed responsibly.
